# Lesson 01 landed, but the anchoring concept needed its own treatment

Sheriff completed Lesson 01 and demonstrated the discrimination skill: he worked the
feedback-vs-advice material closely enough to single out the fourth self-scoring check
("did you locate it in time?") as the one thing he did not already understand, and asked for
it to be expanded rather than skimmed. He then read the revision request back as a
content gap, not a preference — evidence he is reading for *mechanism*, not for completion.

## Evidence

- Reported that the advice/feedback distinction was clear enough that the 12-item quiz and the
  rewrite drill felt like confirmation rather than new learning.
- Identified the temporal-anchoring check as the single unfamiliar idea — precisely the concept
  the original lesson had introduced in one line and never justified.
- Reported a usability failure that caused wasted effort: prompts hidden in a `textarea`
  `placeholder`, requiring copy-paste to keep the question visible. Fixed by building the
  `.rewrite` component (one prompt, one field, per item).

## Implications

- **New floor:** feedback-vs-advice discrimination can be assumed from here. Do not re-teach
  it; build on it. Lesson 02 can go straight to *what to look for*.
- **Do not introduce an idea in a checklist or a passing clause.** If it is worth naming, it is
  worth a mechanism and a drill. Sheriff audits the list items — anything that survived as a
  bullet without explanation will be found and rejected.
- **He wants the "why" at research depth, not Toastmasters depth.** The response to "expand on
  this" was satisfied by going to Kluger & DeNisi (1996), Shute (2008) and Wisniewski et al.
  (2020) and giving the attention hierarchy as the mechanism. Plan on doing this for
  voice, body language and structure too — find the perceptual/linguistic research behind each
  Toastmasters criterion rather than restating the criterion.
- **Depth is safe.** Long lessons with several stacked drills did not overwhelm. The failure
  mode to avoid is *shallowness*, not length.
