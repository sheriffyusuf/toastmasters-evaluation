# Mission: Evaluating Speeches at Toastmasters

## Why

Sheriff wants to stand up in a Toastmasters club meeting and deliver evaluations that
genuinely move a speaker forward — and to become a genuinely eloquent speaker in the
process. Evaluation is the highest-leverage role in a club: it forces analytical
listening, structured thinking and unrehearsed eloquence at the same time. Getting good
at it makes every other speaking skill visible and teachable.

## Success looks like

- Walk into a club meeting as the assigned evaluator, watch a 5–7 minute speech, and
  deliver a 2–3 minute verbal evaluation that the speaker calls useful.
- Name from memory every criterion a Toastmasters evaluator assesses, and explain what
  each one *adds* to a speech and why it is essential — not just list them.
- Place any speech on the official 1–5 scale (Developing → Exemplary) and defend the
  placement with observed behaviour.
- Distinguish an adequate evaluation from a great one from an excellent one using the
  Evaluation Contest Judge's Guide criteria (Analytical Quality, Recommendations,
  Technique, Summation).
- Rewrite "advice" into "feedback" instantly, on the spot, without triggering
  defensiveness.
- Give an evaluation that is itself a well-crafted 2–3 minute speech: clear structure,
  vocal variety, purposeful movement, memorable close.

## Constraints

- Wants depth over brevity. Explicitly does not want summaries, shortcuts or
  "good enough" explanations. Every element needs its *why*.
- Wants every lesson to include exercises that create a real feedback loop.
- Learns by understanding mechanisms, not by memorising slogans.
- Cares about eloquence as an outcome, not just correctness of feedback.

## Out of scope (for now)

- Speechwriting as a standalone craft (covered only as far as needed to *recognise* it
  in someone else's speech).
- Other club meeting roles (Timer, Ah-Counter, Grammarian, Toastmaster of the day)
  except where they intersect with evaluating.
- Club/district administration, officer roles, contest organisation.
- Judging at Area/Division level as a certified judge (aspirational, not current).
