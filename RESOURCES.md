# Evaluating Speeches at Toastmasters — Resources

## Knowledge

### Primary — official Toastmasters International documents

- [Booklet: _Effective Evaluation_ (Item 202, Rev. 6/2011)](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/department-documents/education-documents/202-effective-evaluation.pdf)
  The canonical short text on evaluating. Covers the evaluator's role, how to prepare,
  the "I" message rules, feedback vs. advice in spirit, delivery of the evaluation, and
  follow-up with speaker and audience. **Use for:** anything about the *conduct* of an
  evaluation. Local copy: `.sources/202-effective-evaluation.txt`

- [Manual: _Fundamentals of Public Speaking_ (Toastmasters, current)](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/department-documents/club-documents/fundamentals-of-public-speaking/fundamentals-of-public-speaking-english.pdf)
  The current introductory manual. Speech structure (opening / body / conclusion),
  transitions, common opening mistakes. **Use for:** the content side of what an
  evaluator judges. Local copy: `.sources/fundamentals.txt`

- [Project: _8101 Ice Breaker_ — including full evaluation form and criteria](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/pathways/2021-updates/8101-ice-breaker.pdf)
  Contains the **base Pathways rubric** used across most speeches: Clarity, Vocal
  Variety, Eye Contact, Gestures, Audience Awareness, Comfort Level, Interest. **Use
  for:** the seven criteria that recur constantly. Local copy: `.sources/8101-ice-breaker.txt`

- [Project: _8103 Writing a Speech With Purpose_ — evaluation form and criteria](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/pathways/2021-updates/8103-writing-a-speech-with-purpose.pdf)
  Adds the **content** criteria: Specific Purpose, General Speech Purpose, Interest and
  Purpose. Also lists the eight organisational structures (chronological, topical,
  spatial, causal, comparative, problem/solution, particular-general-particular).
  **Use for:** judging structure and purpose. Local copy: `.sources/8103-writing-a-speech-with-purpose.txt`

- [Project: _8104 Introduction to Vocal Variety and Body Language_ — evaluation form, criteria and Speech Profile](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/pathways/2021-updates/8104-vocal-variety-and-body-language.pdf)
  The deepest official treatment of delivery criteria. Adds **Unintentional Movement**
  and **Purposeful Movement** to the rubric, defines the four gesture types
  (descriptive, emphatic, suggestive, prompting), and contains the **Speech Profile**
  scales for voice (loudness, pitch, voice quality, articulation, timing/rate, vocal
  variety). **Use for:** all delivery criteria. Local copy: `.sources/8104-vocal-variety.txt`

- [Project: _8100 Evaluation and Feedback_ — includes the advice/feedback table](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/pathways/2021-updates/8100-evaluation-and-feedback.pdf)
  The Level 1 project that teaches evaluating. Contains the definitive **Advice vs
  Feedback** pairing table and the "behaviour not person" rule. **Use for:** the core
  feedback technique. Local copy: `.sources/8100-eval-feedback.txt`

- [Booklet: _Your Speaking Voice_ (Item 199)](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/department-documents/education-documents/199-your-speaking-voice.pdf)
  Voice production mechanics: breath, phonation, resonation. Defines the five qualities
  of a good speaking voice (pleasant, natural, dynamic, expressive, easily heard).
  Gives the 120–160 wpm optimum rate, the three conditions of clear articulation, and
  inflection examples. **Use for:** diagnosing *why* a voice fails, and voice exercises.
  Local copy: `.sources/199-your-speaking-voice.txt`

- [Form: _Evaluation Contest Judge's Guide and Ballot_ (Item 1179, Rev. 12/2025)](https://content.toastmasters.org/image/upload/1179-evaluation-contest-judges-guide-and-ballot.pdf)
  **The single best definition of an excellent evaluation.** Scores out of 100:
  Analytical Quality 40, Recommendations 30, Technique 15, Summation 15 — each with
  Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair point bands. **Use for:** the top of the ladder.
  Local copy: `.sources/ballot1179.txt`

### Secondary — high-reputation practitioner writing

- [Article: "The Secret to a Meaningful Evaluation" — Toastmasters Magazine, Apr 2019](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2019/apr/meaningful-evaluation)
  Argues against a mechanical "sandwich of affirmation" and for evaluations with real
  substance. Use for: pushing past the beginner formula.

- [Article: "A Recipe for the Evaluation Sandwich" — Toastmasters Magazine, Nov 2018](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2018/nov2018/29-toastmasters-toolbox)
  States the CRC model (Commend–Recommend–Commend) and its roots in educational
  research on why negative feedback fails to change behaviour.

- [Article: "Expert Advice for Evaluations" — Toastmasters Magazine, Jul 2023](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2023/july/evaluations)
  Practical tips: talk to the speaker beforehand, know the project, use the project's
  own evaluation form.

- [Article: "Controlling Your Pace and Pitch" — Toastmasters Magazine, Jul 2025](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2025/july/controlling--your-pace--and-pitch)
  The insight that **emotion is carried primarily in vowel sounds**, so pace control is
  really emotion control. Use for: explaining pace beyond "too fast / too slow".

- [Article: "The Ins and Outs of Dynamic Speaking" — Toastmasters Magazine, Nov 2023](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2023/nov/dynamic-speaking)
  "Vocal foundation is the cake, vocal variety is the icing." Use for: why variety
  without a solid base fails.

- [Article: "Modulate to Motivate" — Toastmasters Magazine, Jun 2026](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2026/june/modulate-to-motivate)
  Names vocal variety as *intentional* shifting of the voice to create meaning, emotion
  and engagement.

- [Article: "How to Build a Speech" — Toastmasters Magazine, Jun 2021](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2021/june/how-to-build-a-speech)
  Structure, stories and word choice. Includes a concrete failure analysis of an
  over-long opening.

- [Essay: "Want to Win an Evaluation Contest?" — Westside Toastmasters](https://westsidetoastmasters.com/article_reference/want_to_win_evaluation_contest.html)
  Best-known practitioner essay on going beyond the sandwich to contest-level
  evaluations.

- [Blog: Six Minutes — "Speech Analysis #5: Toastmasters Evaluation Contests" (Andrew Dlugan)](https://sixminutes.dlugan.com/speech-evaluation-5-toastmasters-contests/)
  Documents the contest format and note-taking structure in detail.

- [Blog: mannerofspeaking.org — "Speech Evaluations: 10 tips for the Toastmasters Contest"](https://mannerofspeaking.org/2009/05/29/speech-evaluations/)
  John Zimmer's practical contest tips. High-reputation speaker-coach writing.

### Tertiary — the research literature on feedback

These explain the *mechanisms* behind Toastmasters' rules. Use them whenever the question is
“why does that work?” rather than “what does Toastmasters say?”

- [Paper: “The Effects of Feedback Interventions on Performance” — Kluger & DeNisi, _Psychological Bulletin_ 119(2), 1996](https://www.mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Marking%20and%20Feedback/The%20effects%20of%20feedback%20interventions.pdf)
  **The single most important source in this course.** Meta-analysis of 131 studies, 607
  effect sizes, 23,663 observations. Mean effect d = .41, but **over one third of feedback
  interventions decreased performance.** Proposes Feedback Intervention Theory: the attention
  hierarchy of meta-task (self) → task-motivation → task-learning, and the finding that
  “FI effectiveness decreases as attention moves up the hierarchy closer to the self and away
  from the task.” **Use for:** why person-directed feedback fails, why the “I” message rule
  exists, why anchoring works. Local copy: not stored (large); link is stable.

- [Paper: “Focus on Formative Feedback” — Valerie J. Shute, _Review of Educational Research_ 78(1), 2008](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)
  A 26-page review of the entire feedback literature. §3.3 defines feedback specificity
  (via Goodman, Wood & Hendrickx 2004) and catalogues the consequences of vagueness: perceived
  uselessness, frustration, uncertainty, extra cognitive load, reduced motivation. §3.4 covers
  verification vs elaboration (Kulhavy & Stock 1989). Also covers timing, complexity and
  goal orientation. **Use for:** every claim about specificity and about what vagueness costs.

- [Paper: “The Power of Feedback Revisited” — Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie, _Frontiers in Psychology_, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456/)
  Replication across 435 studies, k = 994, N > 61,000. Mean effect d = 0.48, with strong
  heterogeneity — the authors conclude feedback **“cannot be understood as one construct.”**
  **Use for:** establishing that *which kind* of feedback matters more than *how much*.

- [Paper: “The Power of Feedback” — Hattie & Timperley, _Review of Educational Research_ 77(1), 2007](https://assess.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-02/hattietimperley_2007.pdf)
  The canonical framework behind the “visible learning” feedback literature. **Use for:** the
  feed-up / feed-back / feed-forward distinction, if it becomes relevant.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- **Your own club.** The fastest feedback loop available. Ask the VPE to schedule you as
  an evaluator as often as possible, and ask a senior member to watch your evaluation and
  give you feedback on it.
- [r/Toastmasters](https://www.reddit.com/r/Toastmasters/)
  Moderately active. Use for: evaluation text critique, contest questions, "how would you
  evaluate this?" threads.
- **District evaluation workshops and the Evaluation Contest.** Every district conference
  runs evaluation training and the contest is the strongest available benchmark. Watching
  a Division final is worth more than reading ten articles.
- [The Art of Effective Evaluation (Item 251)](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources)
  A 2.5-hour club-run seminar with a workbook. Ask your VPE to schedule it. Excellent for
  practising with immediate feedback.
- [Evaluate to Motivate (Item 292)](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources)
  A 10-minute club presentation from The Successful Club Series. Good as a refresher.
