Evaluating Speeches
at Toastmasters

From “that was a nice speech” to an evaluation the speaker remembers for a year. Built entirely from Toastmasters International's own published criteria.

Start here

Read Lesson 01 — You Are Not a Judge. It takes about twenty minutes to read and twenty-five to practise, and it contains the single highest-leverage habit in the whole discipline. Then Lesson 02 — The Anatomy of a Speech gives you the full map of what you are judging, and the order to fix it in.

Going to a meeting soon? Open the Evaluator's Quick Card — it fits on one phone screen.

LessonsThe course, in order

  • Lesson 01 · Foundations You Are Not a Judge What an evaluation actually is. The feedback-versus-advice distinction, the three ingredients, and how to anchor a comment to a moment the speaker can check. 4 drills. ~45 min with exercises
  • Lesson 02 · Foundations The Anatomy of a Speech The map of everything you are judging: content versus delivery, the seven core criteria and what each one adds, the reconstruction test, and the order of repair. 4 drills. ~50 min with exercises
  • Lesson 03 · Coming next Content: Purpose and Structure General purpose, specific purpose, the eight organisational structures, and how to tell a deliberate structure from an accidental one. not yet written
  • Lesson 04 Voice: Pitch, Tone, Volume, Pace Each component of vocal variety in depth — what it adds, why it is essential, how it fails, and how to name the failure precisely. not yet written
  • Lesson 05 Body Language Posture, stance, position, movement, the four gesture types, eye contact and facial expression — including the two movement criteria most evaluators miss. not yet written
  • Lesson 06 Scoring Honestly Using the 1–5 ladder without clustering at 4s, and keeping mechanics from swallowing the point of the speech. not yet written
  • Lesson 07 Structuring the Evaluation Itself Commend–Recommend–Commend and what lies beyond it. Building an evaluation that is itself a well-made short speech. not yet written
  • Lesson 08 Delivering It The evaluation as a performance: voice, eye contact, the close, and handling the moment when the speaker disagrees. not yet written
  • Lesson 09 The Excellent Tier Contest-level analysis: finding one cause behind three symptoms, and the four criteria that define an excellent evaluation. not yet written

ReferenceFor use, not for reading

Compressed. Designed to be looked up mid-meeting and to print cleanly.

  • Reference 03 · One screen Evaluator's Quick Card The operational checklist. What to do before, during and after the speech, plus the phrase patterns. Open this one at the meeting. read in 3 minutes
  • Reference 02 The Excellence Ladder Good, great and excellent — defined in Toastmasters' own words for both the speech and the evaluation of it. Every rubric level, transcribed. keep open while scoring
  • Reference 01 Glossary of Terms The exact vocabulary this course uses, defined once. Pitch, tone, inflection, articulation, the four gesture types, the eight structures. canonical — never contradicted

NotesHow to use this on a phone

  • Add it to your home screen. In Safari: Share → Add to Home Screen. In Chrome: ⋮ → Add to Home screen. It then opens full-screen like an app.
  • Everything works offline once loaded — no CDN, no fonts to fetch, no analytics. But your quiz answers and drill notes are saved per-device, so they will not follow you between your phone and your laptop.
  • The Quick Card is the one to bookmark. Reading the course builds the skill; the card is what you actually use standing up.
