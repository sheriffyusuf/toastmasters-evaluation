# Depth is not sprawl: the anchoring revision overshot

Sheriff compared the revised Lesson 01 against the previous version and rejected the
revision. His words: it “contains so much fluff and so many section[s that] don't make
sense.” The instruction was not to remove the anchoring concept — that was his own earlier
request — but to restore the **structure and content** of the previous lesson.

## What was in the rejected revision

- A “why any of this matters” passage with Kluger & DeNisi's meta-analysis numbers
  (131 studies, d = .41, one third negative) and the Wisniewski replication.
- The attention hierarchy (meta-task / task-motivation / task-learning) as a fourth
  feedback-vs-advice mechanism.
- A full Section 4 on anchoring: Shute on specificity, a block quote on the costs of
  vagueness, Phye & Sanders, an “on sourcing” meta-note, the four jobs of WHEN, a
  one-off/pattern counting sub-section, a before/after table, and a “when you cannot
  anchor” section.
- An invented six-minute speech (“The Last Bus”) with a timeline, plus a 6-item
  “turn notes into findings” drill built on it.
- 5 drills, 8 sources, a 12-item milestone checklist.

## Evidence

- He named the previous version explicitly as the preferred one, rather than asking for
  targeted edits — a structural rejection, not a factual correction.
- Nothing in the rejected material was *wrong*. The research was accurate and cited. He
  still called it fluff. The objection is to **length without a new mechanism**, and to
  sections that exist to cover an edge case or a related finding.
- This qualifies the implication in `0001-reads-for-mechanism-not-coverage.md` that “he
  wants the why at research depth.” He wants *mechanism* at depth. Citation density is
  not the same thing, and research belongs in the glossary's evidence base.

## Implications

- **Structure is part of the content.** Restore the previous lesson's shape: five sections,
  four drills, four sources, short checklist. Keep it that way for future lessons.
- **One mechanism per idea, explained fully.** Do not add a second literature review to
  prove the first one. If a finding is worth citing, cite it once and move on.
- **Cut edge-case sections from foundations lessons.** “What if you cannot anchor?” is a
  good question, not a good section. It belongs in the ask-teacher list or a later lesson.
- **Anchoring stays, compressed.** The compact subsection (anchor test + three coordinates
  + one counting sentence) satisfies the original request without the research apparatus.
- The index page and NOTES.md had drifted out of sync with the lesson (drill count, time).
  After any lesson revision, update both.
