Body copy. Inline bold, italic, and a citation as sup.cite.Effective Evaluation

Citations

One name per document, and it is never a catalogue number. Item numbers (199, 8104) tell the reader nothing mid-sentence — they are purchase codes. The reader sees Effective Evaluation in the prose and the same string in the marker next to the quotation, so no lookup is needed and nothing has to be decoded.

<sup class="cite"><a href="#s1" title="Effective Evaluation (Item 202), p. 5">Effective Evaluation</a></sup>

<sup class="cite"><a href="#s1" title="…">Vocal Variety and Body Language</a>
  <span class="sep">·</span>
  <a href="#s2" title="…">Your Speaking Voice</a></sup>

Rules, in order of how often they are broken:

  1. Names. An item's short title (Effective Evaluation, Evaluation and Feedback, Your Speaking Voice, Ice Breaker, Writing a Speech With Purpose, Fundamentals of Public Speaking, Vocal Variety and Body Language for Item 8104, Evaluation Contest Judge's Guide and Ballot). Magazine articles use their real title in quotes; research papers use author–year (Shute 2008).
  2. Never an item number in reading text. Numbers appear only in the .sources list at the foot of the page and inside title attributes. Check every page in lessons/ and reference/ with grep -o 'Item [0-9]\{3,4\}' — each hit must be in one of those two places. (This gallery is the exception: it names item numbers deliberately, to document the mapping.)
  3. Pages go in the title, never in the marker text. The marker's job is “which document”; the page is what you want only once you have decided to check.
  4. One marker per source per paragraph, at that source's last quotation; fold the pages into one title (pp. 3, 5). Two different sources in one paragraph keep two markers.
  5. In prose, use <i> for a work title and reserve <em> for terms and criteria, so a title can be told from an emphasised word in the markup even though both render italic.
  6. Repeat mentions take pronouns, not a second name. Name the work when the referent needs establishing; after that write “the two manuals”, “it”, “the project”.

There is no masthead key. Markers are self-describing, so the old .citekey decoder line was removed along with its CSS.

A blockquote attribution does not take a marker — the attribution is the citation, so link the work title and leave the item number out:

<cite>Toastmasters International, <a href="#s1"><i>Effective Evaluation</i></a>, p. 3</cite>

Rendered, the marker and the attribution look like this:

Effective Evaluation insists the comment must be specific.Effective Evaluation

A pull quote. Use it when the exact words of a source matter more than your paraphrase.
